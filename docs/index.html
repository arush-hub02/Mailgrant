The Fundamental Problem

Why Sharing Your Password Is a Disaster

When someone needs to log into Claude, AWS, or Notion, sharing your email password exposes your entire digital existence.

The Old Way: Sharing Passwords

Total Inbox & Identity Exposure

Sending your Gmail password or 2FA recovery codes hands the other person the master key to your entire digital life.

  • Banking & Taxes Exposed: Recipient can read Chase/Wells Fargo balances, tax forms, and PayPal statements.
  • Full Google Account Compromised: Grants unrestricted access to Google Drive files, Photos, and location history.
  • Account Takeovers: Recipient can initiate "Forgot Password" on your other services and reset them.
  • Painful Revocation: Revoking access forces you to change your Google password and re-authenticate dozens of personal devices.
The Mailgrant Way: Zero-Trust Proxy

Surgical Extraction · Zero Password

Grant temporary access to specific services with strict edge filtering and automatic code extraction.

  • No Password Sharing: Connects via read-only Google OAuth. Recipient never receives your Google password.
  • Personal Mail 100% Blocked: Unapproved senders (banks, personal contacts, family) are rejected immediately at the edge.
  • Body-Stripping Engine: Full message bodies are discarded. The recipient sees only the 6-digit OTP code or sign-in link.
  • Instant 1-Click Revocation: Revoke any key instantly or set it to auto-expire after 15m, 24h, or 30 days.

Security Architecture

Built on Zero-Trust Principles

Every boundary is enforced on the server. Your Google password and sensitive personal emails are never exposed.

Zero Password Sharing

Never give away your Gmail credentials. Recipients only receive temporary, scoped access tokens managed by the proxy.

Strict Sender Whitelisting

Only matching authenticated emails pass through. Bank alerts, personal correspondence, and unapproved services are rejected at the edge.

Code-Only Retention

For 2FA verification rules, the application strips out the entire email body and retains only the extracted OTP code digits.

Automatic Expiry

Messages automatically expire after 15 to 60 minutes. Once expired, message content is completely inaccessible.

Multi-Mailbox Isolation
